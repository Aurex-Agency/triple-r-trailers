Dealer Stock or a Factory Order: What to Ask Before Buying

By Triple R Trailers · October 27, 2026

Find a Triple R Dealer

Start with your requirements and your deadline

A trailer on a dealer lot gives you a specific unit to inspect. An order request starts with a proposed configuration that needs confirmation. The better route depends on whether a suitable unit is available, which features matter and how your schedule fits the selling dealer’s current process.

Write down the load, usable-space requirements and towing information before calling. Separate essential requirements from preferences. A different color may be acceptable; an unsuitable opening, capacity or loading arrangement is not something to overlook because a unit is available.

When comparing dealer stock

Ask the dealer to confirm that the specific trailer is still available and describe its configuration. Request current photos or arrange an inspection. Check the unit’s labels, usable dimensions, loading equipment and included accessories rather than relying only on a category name.

Before traveling, confirm the lot location, opening hours and who will help you. Ask whether any reservation arrangements apply and get the terms directly from the seller. A website photo or listing does not by itself reserve a trailer or establish today’s stock.

When discussing an order

Give the dealer a written requirements list and ask which configurations can currently be quoted. Identify standard equipment and requested options separately. Availability of a feature on another trailer is not confirmation that it is available on this build.

Ask who confirms the order specification, how changes are handled and when the dealer can provide an updated timing estimate. Keep a copy of the agreed description. Avoid arranging a time-sensitive job around an unconfirmed build or arrival date.

Compare prices on the same basis

Request an itemized description of what each quote includes. Compare the trailer configuration, accessories, any delivery arrangements and other applicable charges. Ask the seller to explain payment, deposit, cancellation and warranty terms in writing.

This guide does not set dealer terms or promise a factory lead time. A lower headline price may describe a different configuration or collection arrangement. Resolve those differences before deciding which quote meets your requirements.

Plan collection or delivery with the seller

Ask who coordinates pickup or delivery and what needs to be ready beforehand. Confirm the destination or collection location, contact person and the documents the seller will provide. If you are crossing a state line, check the applicable requirements with the relevant authorities.

Review the towing setup and loading arrangements before collecting the trailer. Ask the dealer to explain the unit’s features and provide the applicable instructions. Do not assume an earlier phone conversation has resolved every compatibility or documentation question.

Send a request the dealer can act on

Include the trailer category, cargo dimensions and weight, vehicle information, town or ZIP code, required features and preferred timing. Say whether you are considering current stock, an order or either option. If you have a particular listing in mind, identify it.

Use the dealer finder to contact a nearby seller, or ask the factory for help locating the right contact. The next step is a confirmed conversation about an actual unit or available build, not a promise that a particular trailer, price or delivery date exists.
