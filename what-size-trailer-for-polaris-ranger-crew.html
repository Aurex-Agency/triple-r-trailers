What Size Trailer Do You Need for a Polaris Ranger Crew?

By Triple R Trailers · September 25, 2026

Choose a trailer from the measurements and operating weight of your exact Ranger Crew, then check usable deck space, ramps, and towing limits. The vehicle name and a nominal trailer length are not enough to guarantee a fit.

Our utility gallery shows a crew UTV on a Triple R tandem trailer. It is a useful example of the type of job these trailers do, but different model years, cab packages, tires, and accessories can change the space and capacity needed. Start with the vehicle you own.

Measure the complete side-by-side

Write down overall length, widest point, and height with accessories fitted. Include bumpers, spare-tire carriers, racks, mirrors, and anything else that extends beyond the original body. Check the manufacturer's specifications for your exact model and confirm modifications separately.

Use the operating weight with installed accessories and the cargo that will travel. A brochure dry weight may not represent the vehicle as you load it. Bring the owner's manual and your measurement list to the dealer.

Will a 12-, 14-, or 16-foot trailer work?

Compare usable deck dimensions with the vehicle's full footprint. Allow working space to reach appropriate tie-down points and close the gate as intended. Check width between obstructions, not only outside trailer width. A dovetail or angled rear section is not the same as an equal length of level deck.

A longer deck may provide room for equipment or an easier loading arrangement. It also changes storage and maneuvering needs. Triple R lists a 7x16 tandem utility trailer; ask a dealer to check your particular machine against that build rather than assuming every crew model fits.

A 12-foot trailer that seems close on paper deserves a physical fit check before purchase. Do not plan to rely on an open gate or unsupported overhang to make an unsuitable trailer work. If you want an enclosed trailer, also measure the rear opening and clearance through the entire loading path.

Check the loading path and ramps

Deck space is only part of the fit. Check ramp width, rating, attachment method, approach angle, and ground clearance. A ramp can have a lower rating than the trailer carrying it. Ask the dealer to explain the rated loading arrangement and follow the vehicle and trailer instructions.

Our utility loading videos show real trailers and equipment. Use them to see the layout and ask better questions; they do not replace model-specific loading and securement instructions.

Check payload and the tow vehicle together

Available cargo capacity starts with the trailer's labeled GVWR minus its actual empty weight. Include accessories and any additional equipment carried on the trailer. Confirm the exact build's ratings rather than deriving them from the number of axles.

The towing setup also has limits. Check the tow vehicle's manual and labels for its specific configuration, including applicable towing, payload, axle, hitch, and combined-weight ratings. Passengers and gear in the tow vehicle matter too. Having the correct ball size alone does not establish towing compatibility.

Plan for securement access

Leave access to the vehicle manufacturer's specified attachment points. Use suitable rated equipment and follow the exact model's transport instructions. Do not assume another owner's strap arrangement is correct for your machine. Polaris provides RANGER transport guidance and model-specific resources.

Think about your regular routine: where you load, how you reach the straps, where the trailer is stored, and what else travels. These practical checks can make one otherwise suitable trailer much easier to use than another.

Bring this checklist to your dealer

  • UTV make, model, year, and accessories.
  • Measured overall length, width, height, and operating weight.
  • Additional equipment and its weight.
  • Tow vehicle configuration and relevant ratings.
  • Storage space and normal loading conditions.
  • Your ZIP code and desired purchase timing.

Ask for written confirmation of usable deck dimensions, empty weight, GVWR, and ramp rating for the proposed trailer. For other loads, our general trailer sizing guide helps you work through the same decision.
